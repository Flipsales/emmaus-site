About Emmaus: Walk with God

Emmaus: Walk with God is a daily discipleship app for iPhone, iPad, and Android, published by Disciple Maker, LLC. It includes the whole Bible and is built around what happens around your reading: a morning word, prayer for the people you love, an evening look back at your day, and a friend to walk with.

The name comes from Luke 24, where two friends walked the road to Emmaus with the risen Jesus. He opened the Scriptures to them on the road, and they recognized Him when He broke bread. Emmaus is built around that road and a simple aim: never walk through another day unaware of God's presence.

Scripture and daily rhythms

Ask about a verse and be pointed back to the passage. You can type, or speak and hear a reply out loud. Read the whole Bible one chapter at a time, beginning with Proverbs, then the New Testament, then everything else. Today's chapter is simply the next one — no deadline, no streaks, and no such thing as behind. The app includes the Berean Standard, King James, and American Standard translations, and every chapter can be read aloud.

Each day has a simple shape: a short morning word grounded in Scripture, prayer for the people you love, and an evening look back with God. You can invite a friend, a mentor, or someone you're discipling to walk with you. Walking partners see only what you choose to share. Every connection is invited, mutual, and can be ended by either of you at any time.

Emmaus is a tool. The Bible is the source. The Holy Spirit is the teacher. It does not replace your Bible, your church, a pastor, or a trusted believer. The walk is private: there is no public feed, no followers, and no ads. You can export your data or delete your account in the app.

Emmaus is available for iPhone and iPad on the App Store, and for Android on Google Play. Eligible new subscribers may receive a free trial through Apple; Apple shows the trial length, eligibility, price, and billing terms before you confirm. On Apple, US plans are $8.99 a month or $69.99 a year.

Looking for a place to begin? Start with Scripture and a simple prayer.
