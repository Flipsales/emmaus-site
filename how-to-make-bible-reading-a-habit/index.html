How to make Bible reading a habit

To make Bible reading a habit, make it small enough to do on your worst day, attach it to something you already do every day (like your morning coffee), and keep your Bible where you'll see it. Read at the same time and place when you can, and when you miss a day, simply pick up with the next chapter instead of starting over. Consistency grows from grace and small steps, not from guilt.

If you have started and stopped more times than you can count, you are in good company. Many sincere Christians know the cycle: a fresh start, a good week, a busy stretch, then the quiet guilt of an unopened Bible. The good news is that this cycle can be broken, and it usually starts by asking less of yourself, not more.

Remember why you're reading

Habits last longer when they are tied to something you love, not just something you think you should do. We don't read the Bible to earn God's approval. In Christ we already have His love. We read because He speaks there, and because we need it the way we need food. Jesus said:

“But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.”

Matthew 4:4 (KJV)

And Peter wrote, “As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby” (1 Peter 2:2). Reading is how we're fed. Keeping that in view turns a chore into an invitation.

1. Start smaller than you think you should

One chapter a day is plenty. On a hard day, one paragraph or even one verse is better than nothing. A small habit you keep will do far more than a big plan you abandon. You can always read more on days when you want to. Our one-chapter-a-day reading plan is built on this idea.

2. Tie it to something you already do

Pick a moment that already happens every day and attach your reading to it:

  • With your first cup of coffee or tea
  • Right after you get your kids to school
  • On your lunch break
  • Before you look at your phone in the morning
  • Right before bed

The specific time matters less than having one. "I'll read sometime today" usually becomes "I didn't read today."

3. Make it easy to begin

Put your Bible where you'll see it: on the kitchen table, on your pillow, next to the coffee maker. If you read on your phone, put the app on your home screen. Decide the night before what you'll read next, so there's no decision to make in the morning. The fewer steps between you and the page, the better.

4. Have a simple pattern

Knowing what to do each time makes it easier to start. A simple pattern:

  1. Pray a one-line prayer for understanding.
  2. Read one chapter.
  3. Write down one verse that stood out.
  4. Pray about it for a minute.

That's it. For a fuller rhythm, see how to have a quiet time.

5. Expect to miss days, and plan for it

You will miss days. Everyone does. What matters is what happens next. Here is the single most helpful rule for building a lasting habit: never try to catch up. Don't read three chapters tomorrow to make up for two missed days. Don't start the plan over. Just read the next chapter.

Guilt is a poor motivator. It tends to make us avoid the very thing we feel guilty about. God's mercy is a better one:

“22It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. 23They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23 (KJV)

Every morning is a new morning to open the Word again.

6. Read with someone

Habits are easier to keep with company. Ask a friend, spouse or small-group member to read the same chapters as you. A quick text such as "Read John 4 today, verse 14 got me" keeps you both going. Paul encouraged believers to let the word of Christ dwell in them richly, “teaching and admonishing one another” (Colossians 3:16). Scripture was never meant to be a solo project.

7. Notice the fruit, not the streak

Counting days can be motivating for a while, but streaks have a downside: when one breaks, it's easy to feel like quitting altogether. Instead of tracking a number, notice what's changing. Are you more patient? Do verses come to mind during the day? Are you praying more naturally? That's the real fruit, and it grows slowly. Paul wrote, “let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not” (Galatians 6:9).

When you've fallen off for a long time

If it has been weeks or months, don't wait for a perfect Monday or a new year. Open your Bible today, read one chapter, and pray something honest: "Lord, I'm back. Help me keep going." There's no penalty for the gap. Jesus' invitation still stands: “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest” (Matthew 11:28).

Questions people ask

How long does it take to build a Bible reading habit?

There is no fixed number of days. It depends on the person and their circumstances. Keeping the habit small, tying it to a daily routine and restarting quickly after a miss all help it last.

What is the best time of day to read the Bible?

The best time is the one you can keep. Many people read in the morning, but lunch breaks and evenings work well too. Choose a time you can attach to something you already do daily.

What should I do if I miss several days of Bible reading?

Don't try to catch up or start over. Simply read the next chapter. Missing days is normal, and God's mercies are new every morning.

How much should I read each day to stay consistent?

Start with one chapter a day, or less on a hard day. A small amount you read consistently is better than a big plan you abandon.
