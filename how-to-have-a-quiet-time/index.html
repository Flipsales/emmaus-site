How to have a quiet time with God

To have a quiet time, set aside a few minutes each day, ideally at the same time and in the same place, to be alone with God. Start by quieting your heart and praying, read a short passage of Scripture, reflect on what it says, write down a thought or a verse, and close by praying about what you read and what is ahead. Ten to fifteen minutes is a wonderful start.

"Quiet time" isn't a phrase you'll find in the Bible, but the practice is all through it. It's simply a regular time to meet with God through His word and prayer.

Jesus made time to be alone with the Father

Even Jesus, in the middle of crowds and needs, regularly stepped away to pray:

“And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.”

Mark 1:35 (KJV)

If Jesus needed time alone with His Father, so do we. A quiet time isn't a religious duty to check off. It's a chance to slow down, listen and remember that God is with us.

There is a well-known story in Luke 10 about two sisters, Martha and Mary. Martha was busy serving, while Mary “sat at Jesus’ feet, and heard his word” (Luke 10:39). When Martha complained, Jesus answered gently:

“41And Jesus answered and said unto her, Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: 42But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her.”

Luke 10:41–42 (KJV)

A quiet time is choosing that good part, even for a few minutes.

Step 1: Choose a time and a place

Pick a time you can keep most days. Many people prefer the morning, before the day gets loud. The psalmist wrote, “My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD” (Psalm 5:3). But if mornings are chaotic, a lunch break or the evening can work just as well.

Choose a place that's as quiet as you can manage: a chair by a window, the kitchen table before others wake up, your parked car. Having the same spot each day helps your mind settle faster.

Step 2: Gather a few simple things

  • A Bible (paper or app)
  • A notebook or journal and a pen
  • A cup of coffee or tea, if that helps

Put your phone on Do Not Disturb, or out of reach if you read from a paper Bible.

Step 3: Quiet your heart

Take a breath. Sit still for a moment. Psalm 46:10 says:

“Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalm 46:10 (KJV)

Then pray a short, honest prayer. You might say, "Lord, I'm here. Quiet my mind. Help me hear You in Your word." If something heavy is on your heart, tell Him about it right away.

Step 4: Read a short passage

Read one chapter, or even a smaller section. Read it slowly. If a verse catches your attention, stop and read it again. It's better to sit with a few verses than to rush through many. If you don't know what to read, follow a simple plan like our one-chapter-a-day Bible reading plan, or start with a Gospel or Psalms.

Step 5: Reflect and write

Ask yourself a few questions about what you read:

  • What does this passage say about God?
  • What does it say about me or about people?
  • Is there a promise to hold on to, or a command to follow?
  • How might this shape my day?

Write down one verse and one sentence of reflection. Some people like the SOAP method: Scripture, Observation, Application, Prayer. We explain it in how to study the Bible for beginners.

Step 6: Pray

Turn what you read into prayer. Thank God for what you saw in His word. Ask for help to live it today. Then pray for the people in your life and the needs on your heart. The Lord's Prayer is a beautiful outline if you're not sure what to say. See our guide on how to pray.

Step 7: Carry one thing into your day

Before you get up, pick one thing to take with you: a verse to repeat, a person to encourage, or a small step of obedience. This keeps your quiet time from being separate from the rest of your life.

A simple 15-minute quiet time

  • 1 minute: Be still and pray a short prayer.
  • 5 to 7 minutes: Read one chapter or passage slowly.
  • 3 minutes: Write down one verse and one thought.
  • 3 to 4 minutes: Pray about what you read and for the people on your heart.
  • 1 minute: Choose one thing to carry into your day.

Ending the day with God

Some Christians also take a few minutes at night to look back over the day with God. This practice, often called the examen, asks simple questions: Where did I notice God today? Where did I struggle? What am I thankful for? It's a gentle way to close the day and rest.

When your quiet time feels dry

Some days, your quiet time will feel rich. Other days, it'll feel like nothing happened. That's normal. Faithfulness matters more than feelings. God's word works in us even when we don't sense it right away. Keep showing up. If you miss days, don't give up or try to catch up. Simply begin again tomorrow. Questions people ask

What is a quiet time with God?

A quiet time is a regular time, often daily, set aside to be alone with God through reading Scripture and prayer.

How long should a quiet time be?

Ten to fifteen minutes is a good start. Some people spend longer, but consistency matters more than length.

What should I read during my quiet time?

A short passage or one chapter is enough. Many people follow a reading plan, or start with a Gospel or the Psalms.

Is it better to have a quiet time in the morning or at night?

Either works. Many people prefer the morning, as in Psalm 5:3, but the best time is one you can keep most days.

What if my quiet time feels dry?

That is normal. Keep showing up, read slowly, and pray honestly. God's word works in us even when we do not feel it right away.
