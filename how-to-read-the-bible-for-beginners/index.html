How to read the Bible for beginners

To read the Bible as a beginner, choose a good starting book (a Gospel like John is ideal), read one chapter at a time, and read it slowly. Pray before you start, notice one thing that stands out, write down one question, and come back the next day. You don't need special training, a perfect plan or a lot of time. You need a starting place and a small, steady rhythm.

That is really the whole method. The rest of this guide simply unpacks it, so the Bible feels less like a test you might fail and more like a voice you are learning to hear.

Start with the right expectations

The Bible is a library of 66 books: history, poetry, letters, prophecy, wise sayings and the four Gospels about Jesus. Some parts will move you on the first read. Some will confuse you. Both are normal. Christians who have read the Bible for decades still find passages they don't fully understand.

It also helps to remember why we read. The Bible is not mainly a rulebook or a puzzle. It is God's word to us, and its center is Jesus. The psalmist put it this way:

“Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.”

Psalm 119:105 (KJV)

A lamp doesn't light the whole road at once. It lights the next step. That's a good picture of beginning.

Step 1: Pick a starting place

If you are new, start with one of the Gospels. John was written “that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God” (John 20:31). Mark is shorter and quicker. Proverbs is another gentle option because its chapters are short and practical. We go into more detail in where to start reading the Bible.

Step 2: Pray before you read

You don't need polished words. A short, honest prayer is enough:

“Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law.”

Psalm 119:18 (KJV)

Or in your own words: "God, help me understand what I read and take one step toward you today." Jesus promised that the Holy Spirit “shall teach you all things” (John 14:26). Asking for that help is a good way to begin.

Step 3: Read one chapter, slowly

One chapter is enough. Most take only a few minutes. Read it at a normal pace, without stopping to look things up. If you have time, read it a second time more slowly. You will be surprised what you notice the second time that you missed the first.

If reading is hard for you, or your hands are busy, listen to the chapter read aloud. Hearing Scripture is as old as Scripture itself. Paul wrote, “faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God” (Romans 10:17).

Step 4: Notice one thing

When you finish, ask a few simple questions:

  • What happens in this chapter?
  • What does it show me about God?
  • What does it show me about people, or about me?
  • Is there anything here to trust, obey, thank God for or turn away from?

You don't need to answer all of them. Pick one. Underline a verse, or write it on a card.

Step 5: Write down one question

Some chapters will raise more questions than they answer. That is fine. Write down one. Over time, you'll notice the Bible answering many of its own questions as you keep reading. For the ones that stay, talk with a pastor or a trusted believer. Our guide on how to read the Bible and understand it has more on working through hard passages.

Step 6: Carry it into your day

James wrote, “be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only” (James 1:22). Reading is meant to shape how we live. Before you close your Bible, choose one small thing to carry into the day: a verse to remember, a person to pray for, a kind word to say. Then turn what you read into a short prayer.

Step 7: Come back tomorrow

The most important step is the next day. A chapter a day adds up. There are 1,189 chapters in the Bible, so reading one a day takes you through the whole Bible in a little over three years. If you miss a day, don't try to catch up. Just read the next chapter. Our Bible reading plan for beginners is built on exactly this idea.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Trying to do too much. Four chapters a day is a great goal for some people, but for many beginners it turns reading into homework. Start small.
  • Starting over after a miss. You don't owe the Bible a perfect record. Pick up where you left off.
  • Reading only isolated verses. Single verses are precious, but reading whole chapters keeps them in context.
  • Reading alone forever. Invite a friend to read the same chapters, or find a church small group. Questions are easier to carry together.

Questions people ask

What is the best way for a beginner to read the Bible?

Start with a Gospel such as John, read one chapter a day slowly, pray before you read, write down one thing that stood out and one question, and come back the next day.

How much of the Bible should I read each day?

One chapter a day is a good start for most beginners. Most chapters take only a few minutes, and a small amount you actually read is better than a large amount you rush.

Do I need a study Bible to start?

No. A study Bible can be helpful later, but you can begin with any Bible translation you understand.

What should I do when I don't understand a passage?

Read it again, write down your question, and keep reading. Many questions are answered later in Scripture. Bring the ones that stay to a pastor or a trusted believer.
