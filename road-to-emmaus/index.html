What happened on the road to Emmaus (Luke 24)

On the road to Emmaus, two of Jesus' followers were walking from Jerusalem to a village called Emmaus on the day He rose from the dead, sad and confused about His crucifixion. The risen Jesus came alongside them, but they did not recognize Him. As they walked, He opened the Scriptures to them, showing how Moses and all the prophets pointed to Him, and their hearts burned within them. When they reached the village, they urged Him to stay, and they recognized Him when He broke bread with them (Luke 24:13-35).

It's one of the most beloved stories in the Bible, and it's the story our app is named after. Here is what happens, step by step, and what it can mean for us.

The setting: the same day Jesus rose

Luke 24 begins early on the first day of the week, when women came to Jesus' tomb and found it empty. Angels told them He was alive. They reported it to the apostles, but the apostles did not believe them (Luke 24:1-11).

Then Luke continues:

“13And, behold, two of them went that same day to a village called Emmaus, which was from Jerusalem about threescore furlongs. 14And they talked together of all these things which had happened.”

Luke 24:13–14 (KJV)

Threescore furlongs is about seven miles. The exact location of the village is uncertain today, but the walk itself is the heart of the story.

A stranger joins them

“15And it came to pass, that, while they communed together and reasoned, Jesus himself drew near, and went with them. 16But their eyes were holden that they should not know him.”

Luke 24:15–16 (KJV)

Jesus asked what they were discussing, and they stopped, sad. One of them, named Cleopas, was surprised that this traveler hadn't heard what had happened in Jerusalem (Luke 24:17-18). The other disciple is not named.

They told Him about Jesus of Nazareth, a prophet “mighty in deed and word before God and all the people”, and how He had been condemned and crucified. Then they said some of the saddest words in the Gospels:

“But we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel: and beside all this, to day is the third day since these things were done.”

Luke 24:21 (KJV)

"We trusted." Their hope had died with Jesus. They had even heard the women's report of the empty tomb and the angels, but they did not know what to make of it (Luke 24:22-24).

Jesus opens the Scriptures

Then Jesus spoke:

“25Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken: 26Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory? 27And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”

Luke 24:25–27 (KJV)

Imagine hearing that Bible study. Jesus walked them through the Old Testament, beginning with Moses and all the prophets, and showed them how it all pointed to Him, including His suffering and His glory. The cross wasn't the end of their hope. It was what the Scriptures had said must happen.

"Abide with us"

As they came near the village, Jesus acted as if He would keep going. But they didn't want Him to leave:

“But they constrained him, saying, Abide with us: for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent. And he went in to tarry with them.”

Luke 24:29 (KJV)

They didn't know who He was yet, but they knew they wanted Him to stay.

Recognized in the breaking of bread

“30And it came to pass, as he sat at meat with them, he took bread, and blessed it, and brake, and gave to them. 31And their eyes were opened, and they knew him; and he vanished out of their sight.”

Luke 24:30–31 (KJV)

This is the moment of recognition. It wasn't on the road, while He taught them, that they knew Him. It was at the table, when He took the bread, blessed it, broke it and gave it to them. Then their eyes were opened, and they knew Him.

"Did not our heart burn within us?"

Looking back, they realized something had been happening all along:

“And they said one to another, Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?”

Luke 24:32 (KJV)

Even before they recognized Him, His word had been at work in them.

They ran back to Jerusalem

“33And they rose up the same hour, and returned to Jerusalem, and found the eleven gathered together, and them that were with them, 34Saying, The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon. 35And they told what things were done in the way, and how he was known of them in breaking of bread.”

Luke 24:33–35 (KJV)

It was evening when they reached Emmaus, yet they went straight back. Sorrow had turned to joy, and they couldn't keep the news to themselves. While they were still talking, Jesus Himself appeared to the gathered disciples (Luke 24:36). Later, Luke writes, “Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures” (Luke 24:45), much as He had done on the road.

What the road to Emmaus means for us

Jesus walks with us, even when we don't recognize Him

The two disciples were walking away from Jerusalem, discouraged and confused, and Jesus came alongside them. He didn't wait for them to figure things out first. Many of us know what it's like to walk through a day unaware that God is near. This story reminds us that He is.

The whole Bible points to Jesus

Jesus began “at Moses and all the prophets” and showed them “the things concerning himself”. The Old Testament isn't a separate story. It's the beginning of the story that leads to Him. That's why reading all of Scripture, not just favorite verses, matters. Our beginner reading plan walks you through the whole Bible, one chapter a day.

Scripture can stir our hearts before we fully understand

Their hearts burned while He opened the Scriptures, even before they knew who He was. Sometimes God's word works in us long before we can explain what's happening.

Jesus makes Himself known

They didn't recognize Jesus by their own effort. Their eyes were opened. Christ graciously made Himself known to them at the table.

Good news is meant to be shared

The moment they knew Him, they went back to tell others. Encountering the risen Jesus always sends us out.

Questions people ask

What happened on the road to Emmaus?

On the day Jesus rose from the dead, two disciples walking to Emmaus met the risen Jesus but did not recognize Him. He explained how the Scriptures pointed to Him, and they recognized Him when He broke bread with them. They then returned to Jerusalem to tell the other disciples (Luke 24:13-35).

When did the disciples recognize Jesus on the road to Emmaus?

They recognized Him at the table in Emmaus, when He took bread, blessed it, broke it and gave it to them. Luke 24:31 says their eyes were opened and they knew Him.

Who were the two disciples on the road to Emmaus?

One was named Cleopas (Luke 24:18). The Bible does not name the other disciple.

How far was Emmaus from Jerusalem?

Luke says about threescore furlongs, which is about seven miles (Luke 24:13).

What does "did not our heart burn within us" mean?

The disciples realized that while Jesus was talking with them on the road and opening the Scriptures, they had been deeply moved, even before they recognized Him (Luke 24:32).
