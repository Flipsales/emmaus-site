Where to start reading the Bible

Most people should start reading the Bible with one of the Gospels, such as John or Mark, because they tell the story of Jesus, and the rest of the Bible makes the most sense in His light. If you want something short and practical first, Proverbs is a gentle place to begin. You do not need to start at Genesis 1 and push straight through.

The Bible is not one book but a library of 66 books, written over many centuries. That is why opening to page one and reading forward can feel like starting an encyclopedia at "A." Choosing a good starting place is not cheating. It is simply wise.

Why not just start at Genesis?

You can. Genesis is a wonderful book, full of stories you may already half-know: creation, Noah, Abraham, Joseph. The trouble usually comes a little later. After Genesis and the first half of Exodus, many first-time readers walk into long sections of law, temple instructions and genealogies in Leviticus and Numbers. Those chapters matter, but without some context they can feel like a wall. A lot of good intentions end right there.

Starting somewhere else does not mean skipping those books forever. It means meeting Jesus first, so that when you reach the harder parts of the Old Testament you have someone to read them toward. Jesus said of the Old Testament Scriptures, “they are they which testify of me” (John 5:39).

Three good places to begin

1. The Gospel of John

John was written so that readers would come to faith. Near the end, John tells us why he wrote:

“But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.”

John 20:31 (KJV)

John has 21 chapters. At one chapter a day, that is three weeks. It opens with some of the most beautiful lines in Scripture, “In the beginning was the Word” (John 1:1), and it includes conversations with Jesus that people return to for a lifetime, like His talk with Nicodemus in John 3.

2. The Gospel of Mark

Mark is the shortest of the four Gospels, at 16 chapters. It moves quickly, often jumping from one scene to the next, so it suits readers who like a story that keeps going. If John feels deep and reflective, Mark feels like walking alongside Jesus at a brisk pace.

3. Proverbs

Proverbs has 31 chapters, about one for each day of a month. Its short sayings deal with ordinary life: words, work, money, friendship, anger and wisdom. You can understand most of it on a first read, which makes it a kind entry point if the Bible feels intimidating. It also tells you where wisdom starts:

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”

Proverbs 1:7 (KJV)

This is where the reading path in Emmaus begins: Proverbs first, then the New Testament, then the rest of the Old Testament.

What to read after that

Once you have read a Gospel or Proverbs, a simple order that works for many people is:

  • The rest of the New Testament. Read the other Gospels, then Acts, which tells how the early church began, then the letters.
  • Genesis and Exodus. The beginning of the big story, now read with Jesus in view.
  • Psalms. Prayers and songs for every kind of day, including the hard ones.
  • The rest of the Old Testament, a book at a time.

There is no single correct order. Some people read straight through. Some follow a chronological plan. Some read a Gospel, then a book of the Old Testament, back and forth. The best order is the one that keeps you reading. Our Bible reading plan for beginners lays out one simple path, one chapter a day.

Which translation should I use?

Pick one you can understand. The King James Version is beloved and has shaped the English language for centuries, but its older words can slow a new reader down. Many people begin with a modern translation and keep the King James for comparison. The Berean Standard Bible is one modern option. The best translation for you is the one you will actually read.

A simple first week

If you want to start today, here is a gentle first week:

  • Day 1: John 1. Before you read, pray something honest like, "God, help me understand what I read and take one step toward you today."
  • Day 2: John 2.
  • Day 3: John 3.
  • Day 4: John 4.
  • Day 5: John 5.
  • Day 6: John 6.
  • Day 7: Look back. Which verse or moment stayed with you? Write it down.

If a day gets away from you, don't start over or double up. Just read the next chapter the next day. For more on keeping going, see how to make Bible reading a habit.

Questions people ask

Where should a beginner start reading the Bible?

Most beginners do well starting with a Gospel, such as John or Mark, because they tell the story of Jesus. Proverbs is another gentle starting place because its chapters are short and practical.

Should I read the Bible from beginning to end?

You can, but many first-time readers get stuck in Leviticus and Numbers. Starting with a Gospel and coming back to the Old Testament later is a good option. There is no single correct order.

How long is the Gospel of John?

John has 21 chapters. Reading one chapter a day, you will finish it in three weeks.

What Bible translation is best for beginners?

The best translation is one you can understand and will keep reading. Many beginners choose a modern translation, such as the Berean Standard Bible, and use the King James Version for comparison.

What if I miss a day?

Just read the next chapter the next day. You do not need to start over or catch up.
