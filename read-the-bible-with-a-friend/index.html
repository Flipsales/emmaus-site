How to read the Bible with a friend

To read the Bible with a friend, pick one book, read the same chapter on your own each day, write down one thing you noticed and one question, and meet once a week to talk it through and pray. That's it. You don't need a curriculum, a leader's guide or a theology degree. You need a shared passage, a little honesty and a regular time.

Reading with someone is one of the oldest ways Christians have grown. Two friends walked to Emmaus in Luke 24 talking about everything that had happened, and Jesus came alongside them and opened the Scriptures (the road to Emmaus). Faith was never meant to be a solo project.

Who to read with

  • A friend who's also trying to read more. You'll keep each other going.
  • Someone newer to faith. You don't need all the answers to read alongside them. Being a step ahead is enough.
  • A mentor. Ask someone whose faith you respect if they'd read one book with you for a season.
  • Your spouse or a family member. Same passage, same week, one conversation.

Start with one person. Two or three is fine, but a small number leaves room for everyone to talk.

Choose what to read

Pick one book and stay with it. Good first choices:

  • The Gospel of Mark. Sixteen short chapters, fast-moving, centered on Jesus.
  • The Gospel of John. Slower and deeper, and full of conversations people still argue about.
  • Philippians. Four chapters, about joy and contentment, good for a short first round.
  • Proverbs. Thirty-one chapters, easy to discuss, practical every day.

One chapter a day is a pace most people can keep. If you'd like a longer path, see one chapter a day.

A simple rhythm: on your own, then together

During the week, on your own:

  1. Read the chapter. Once is enough. Out loud helps.
  2. Write it down with four headings. A simple method many readers use is S.O.A.P.:
    • Scripture: copy one verse that stood out.
    • Observation: what does it actually say? Who's speaking, to whom, and why?
    • Application: what would it look like to live this today?
    • Prayer: a sentence or two back to God.
  3. Keep one question. Something you didn't understand, or something that bothered you.

Once a week, together (30 to 45 minutes):

  1. Check in. How was your week, honestly?
  2. Share one verse each. Why did it stand out?
  3. Bring your questions. Look at the passage together before reaching for answers.
  4. Name one next step. Small and specific: call your brother, forgive a coworker, read before your phone.
  5. Pray for each other. Keep the requests short and keep them.

Next week, start by asking how that step went. That one question is where most of the growth happens. For more on the study side, see how to study the Bible, and for the prayer side, how to pray.

Questions that work for almost any chapter

  • What does this passage show us about God?
  • What does it show us about people, including us?
  • Is there a promise to trust, a command to obey, or an example to follow or avoid?
  • What's one thing you'd want to remember a week from now?
  • What's hard to accept here?

When neither of you knows the answer

That's normal, and it's fine to say so. A few honest moves:

  • Read the verses around it. Context answers more questions than anything else.
  • Look up the cross-references. Scripture explains Scripture.
  • Ask about the verse. In Emmaus, you can ask about any verse in plain language and be pointed back to the passage. Faithful teachers read hard passages differently, so test what you learn against Scripture together.
  • Bring it to someone wiser. A pastor or a mature believer at your church will be glad you asked.

Staying with it when life gets busy

  • Agree on a time, not just an intention. "Tuesdays at 7" lasts longer than "let's try to meet."
  • Missed a week? Pick up where you are. Don't double up. Just read the next chapter.
  • Keep it small. If meeting in person is hard, a phone call or a short voice message about the week's verse still counts.
  • Celebrate answered prayer. Write it down and look back at it together.

How Emmaus helps you read together

Emmaus is a daily discipleship tool for iPhone, iPad and Android, built to help you understand Scripture and walk it out. It isn't trying to replace the Bible app you already have; many people keep theirs and use Emmaus alongside it.

For reading with someone, Emmaus has walking partners:

  • Invite someone to walk with you: a friend, a mentor, or someone you're discipling.
  • Share the prayers you choose and send a word of courage during the week.
  • Your reflections stay private. Walking partners see only what you choose to share, never your journal, your conversations, or how far you are in the Word.
  • Every connection is invited, mutual, and either of you can end it anytime.

And for the reading itself: the whole Bible one chapter at a time, in the Berean Standard, King James or American Standard translations, with every chapter available to listen to, plus a journal that uses the Scripture, Observation, Application, Prayer headings above. When a passage stops you, you can ask about it right there.

Emmaus supports your church. It doesn't replace your Bible, your church, a pastor or a trusted believer, and it never keeps score: no streaks, no misses, no falling behind.

A first week to try

  • Pick a partner and a book (Mark is a good start).
  • Days 1 to 6: read one chapter a day and write one S.O.A.P. note.
  • Day 7: meet or call for 30 minutes. Share one verse, one question and one prayer request each.

If church feels like too big a step right now, a calm place to start is written for you. And if you're the one doing the discipling, Good Soil Discipleship has a plain guide on how to disciple someone.

Questions people ask

How do you read the Bible with a friend?

Choose one book, read the same chapter on your own each day, write down one thing you noticed and one question, then meet once a week to talk it through and pray for each other.

What book of the Bible should we read together first?

A Gospel is a good start. Mark is short and fast-moving; John is slower and deeper. Philippians works well for a short first round.

What is the S.O.A.P. method?

A simple way to journal a passage under four headings: Scripture (a verse that stood out), Observation (what it says), Application (how to live it) and Prayer.

Is there an app for reading the Bible with a friend?

Emmaus, on iPhone, iPad and Android, lets you invite a walking partner, share the prayers you choose and send encouragement, while your journal and personal reflections stay private.

Can my walking partner see my journal?

No. In Emmaus, walking partners see only what you choose to share, never your journal, your conversations, or how far you are in the Word.

Does Emmaus replace my church or my Bible app?

No. Emmaus supports your church and doesn't replace your Bible, your church, a pastor or a trusted believer. Many people keep their Bible app and use Emmaus alongside it.
