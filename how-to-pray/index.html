How to pray

To pray, simply talk to God honestly, in your own words, as a child talks to a loving father. You can start by thanking Him, telling Him what is on your heart, asking for what you and others need, confessing where you have gone wrong, and trusting Him with the outcome. Jesus gave His followers a pattern for this in the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13), and it is still the best place to learn.

If you feel like you don't know how to pray, you're not alone. Jesus' own disciples asked Him about it:

“And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.”

Luke 11:1 (KJV)

Jesus didn't scold them for asking. He taught them. He will teach you too.

Prayer is a conversation, not a performance

Many people hesitate to pray because they think they need the right words, the right tone or a lot of time. Jesus said the opposite:

“7But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. 8Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.”

Matthew 6:7–8 (KJV)

God isn't impressed by long or fancy prayers. He already knows what you need. Prayer is not about informing God or performing for Him. It is about coming to Him, trusting Him and being with Him. A short, honest sentence is a real prayer.

A pattern from Jesus: the Lord's Prayer

When Jesus taught His disciples to pray, He gave them this:

“9After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. 10Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. 11Give us this day our daily bread. 12And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. 13And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.”

Matthew 6:9–13 (KJV)

You can pray these words exactly as they are. You can also use them as an outline, pausing at each line to pray in your own words:

  • "Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name." Begin with who God is. Thank Him and praise Him. Remember you are talking to a Father who loves you.
  • "Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done." Ask for God's purposes in your life, your family, your church and the world. Surrender your plans to His.
  • "Give us this day our daily bread." Ask for what you need today: provision, strength, wisdom, healing. Nothing is too small to bring to Him.
  • "And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors." Confess your sins honestly and receive His forgiveness. Ask for help to forgive people who have hurt you.
  • "And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil." Ask for protection and strength to resist what pulls you away from God.
  • "For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever." End by trusting Him. He is in charge, and He is good.

Another simple pattern: ACTS

Some Christians find it helpful to remember four parts of prayer with the letters ACTS:

  • Adoration: praise God for who He is.
  • Confession: admit your sins and receive His forgiveness.
  • Thanksgiving: thank Him for what He has done.
  • Supplication: ask for what you and others need.

It's not a formula you must follow, just a reminder of the many ways to talk to God.

Bring your worries to Him

Paul wrote these words to a church facing real hardship:

“6Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. 7And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:6–7 (KJV)

"Be careful for nothing" means don't be anxious about anything. Instead of carrying your worries alone, turn each one into a prayer, with thanks. God promises His peace in return.

When you don't have words

There will be times when you are too tired, sad or confused to know what to say. That's okay. Scripture says the Holy Spirit helps us even then:

“Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.”

Romans 8:26 (KJV)

On those days, you might simply say, "Lord, help." You might pray a psalm, letting its words become yours. Or you might sit quietly in His presence. The psalmist invites us: “pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us” (Psalm 62:8).

Practical tips for a beginner

  • Find a quiet place. Jesus said, “enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret” (Matthew 6:6). A quiet corner, a car or a walk all work.
  • Pray out loud or write it down. Both can help your mind stay focused.
  • Keep a prayer list. Write down people and needs, and note when God answers.
  • Pray Scripture. After reading a chapter, turn one verse into a prayer.
  • Pray through the day. Paul wrote, “Pray without ceasing” (1 Thessalonians 5:17). Short prayers while you drive, work or wait are real prayers too.

A simple prayer to begin

If you want to start right now, you could pray: "God, I don't fully know how to do this, but I want to know You. Thank You for hearing me. Help me understand Your word and take one step toward You today. In Jesus' name, amen."

Questions people ask

How do I start praying to God?

Simply talk to Him honestly in your own words. You can thank Him, tell Him what is on your heart, ask for what you need and trust Him with the outcome. The Lord's Prayer in Matthew 6:9-13 is a helpful pattern.

Do I need special words to pray?

No. Jesus taught that God knows what we need before we ask, and that we are not heard because of many words (Matthew 6:7-8). A short, honest prayer is a real prayer.

How long should I pray?

There is no required length. A one-sentence prayer is real prayer. Many people start with a few minutes a day and find it grows naturally.

What should I do when I don't know what to pray?

Say something simple like "Lord, help," pray a psalm, or sit quietly before God. Romans 8:26 says the Holy Spirit helps us when we do not know what to pray.

What is the ACTS prayer method?

ACTS stands for Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving and Supplication. It is a simple reminder of four ways to talk to God.
