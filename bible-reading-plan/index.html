A Bible reading plan for beginners: one chapter a day

A simple Bible reading plan for beginners is one chapter a day: start with Proverbs, then read the New Testament, then the rest of the Old Testament. There are 1,189 chapters in the Bible, so at one a day you will read the whole thing in a little over three years. There are no dates to keep up with, so you can't fall behind. Today's chapter is simply the next one.

That may sound slow. But if you have ever started a reading plan and stalled, you are not alone, and often the pace is the problem: it turns Scripture into a debt. One chapter you actually read, and think about, is worth more than four you skim out of guilt.

Why one chapter a day works

  • It fits a real day. Most chapters take a few minutes to read. You can read one before work, on a lunch break or in bed.
  • It leaves room to think. When you aren't racing to finish several chapters, you can stop on a verse, read it again and pray about it.
  • Missing a day costs nothing. On a dated plan, missing a week means you are "behind," and the plan starts to feel like something you owe. With one chapter a day and no calendar, there is nothing to catch up on.

The Bible itself paints a picture of steady, daily attention rather than heroic bursts. The person who is blessed in Psalm 1 is the one whose “delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night” (Psalm 1:2). And the Bereans in Acts “searched the scriptures daily” (Acts 17:11).

The plan, step by step

Part 1: Proverbs (31 chapters, about a month)

Proverbs is short, practical and easy to understand on a first read. It deals with everyday life: words, work, friendships, money, anger and wisdom. Its 31 chapters take about a month. It's a gentle way to build the habit before you move into longer books.

Part 2: The New Testament (260 chapters)

Next comes the New Testament, starting with the four Gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke and John. This puts the life, death and resurrection of Jesus in front of you early. Then you read Acts, the story of the early church, followed by the letters and Revelation. At one chapter a day, the New Testament takes about eight and a half months.

Part 3: The rest of the Old Testament (898 chapters)

After the New Testament, go back to Genesis and read the rest of the Old Testament front to back. By now you have the story of Jesus to read it against. Jesus Himself, after His resurrection, showed His friends how the whole Old Testament pointed to Him:

“And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”

Luke 24:27 (KJV)

If you'd rather read in the traditional order from Genesis to Revelation, that works too. The point of this plan is the rhythm, not the sequence.

How this compares with a one-year plan

A one-year plan is a fine goal, and many people love them. To finish the whole Bible in 365 days you need a little over three chapters every day, and more on days with short chapters. If that pace works for you, wonderful. If you have tried a one-year plan and stalled in February, a one-chapter-a-day plan may be the kinder path. You will still read the whole Bible. It just won't be on a deadline.

What to do when a chapter loses you

Some chapters are hard: genealogies, temple measurements, prophecies with unfamiliar names. A few honest ways through:

  1. Read it anyway, once. You don't need to understand every line on the first pass.
  2. Write down one question. Just one.
  3. Ask someone. A pastor, a trusted believer, or a good study resource can help. See how to read the Bible and understand it for more.
  4. Listen instead. Hearing a chapter read aloud can carry you through passages that are hard to read on the page.

No streaks, no scoreboard

Reading Scripture is not a performance. If you miss a day, a week or a season, the next chapter is still just the next chapter. God's mercies, as Jeremiah wrote, “are new every morning” (Lamentations 3:23). Come back without guilt. For more help staying consistent, see how to make Bible reading a habit.

Read it with someone

A plan is easier with a friend. Ask someone to read the same chapters as you, and text each other one verse a week. It doesn't have to be complicated.

A simple way to begin this week

  • Day 1: Proverbs 1. Pray first: "God, help me understand what I read and take one step toward you today."
  • Days 2 to 7: Proverbs 2 through 7, one a day.
  • End of the week: Look back. Which verse stuck with you?

Questions people ask

How long does it take to read the Bible one chapter a day?

The Bible has 1,189 chapters, so reading one a day takes about three years and three months.

What order should I read the Bible in as a beginner?

Any order works. This plan starts with Proverbs, then the New Testament, then the rest of the Old Testament, one chapter a day.

What if I miss a day on my Bible reading plan?

Nothing is lost. There is no deadline and no streak to break. Your next chapter is simply the next one.

Is a one-chapter-a-day plan better than a one-year plan?

Neither is better for everyone. A one-year plan needs about three to four chapters a day. One chapter a day is slower, but many beginners find it easier to keep going.

Does Emmaus replace my Bible or my church?

No. Emmaus is a tool that supports your church. It doesn't replace your Bible, your church, a pastor or a trusted believer.
